## 1. Important Primary Elections Shape America’s Future 

Today, key primary elections are happening in Wisconsin, Minnesota, and South Carolina. These elections are **taking center stage** because they can decide who will run in the big November election. In South Carolina, two Republicans, Sen. Darline Graham and Rep. Ralph Norman, are moving forward to a runoff race. This means they will compete again to be the official candidate. These elections matter because they show how voters feel about the current political issues. People are watching closely because the results could **cause chaos** or change the balance of power in the government. The winners from these primaries will work hard to **advance** their ideas to voters in the general election.

## 2. Immigration Policy Debates Heat Up in Minnesota 

Immigration policy is **taking center stage** again in Minnesota’s primary elections. Many voters are discussing how the federal government handles immigration rules. Recently, President Trump tried to **limit birthright citizenship** by issuing new executive orders. Birthright citizenship means that children born in the US automatically become citizens. This is a very controversial topic because it affects many families and the country’s future. The debate shows a clear divide between people who want stricter immigration control and those who want to support immigrants. What happens next could **advance** new laws or policies about immigration.

## 3. The US Job Market Shows Signs of Weakening 

New reports show that the US job market is **weakening**. In July, the country lost 23,000 jobs, which was a surprise to many experts. This is the first time in a long while that there were more job losses than gains in a month. The summer jobs report is a **double whammy** for workers. This means workers face two problems at the same time: fewer jobs and some previous job numbers being revised lower. These changes may affect how the government plans to support the economy. If the job market continues to get worse, it could **cause chaos** for many families who rely on steady work.

> - **double whammy** — 이중고, 두 가지 문제  
>   _Losing his job and having medical bills was a **double whammy** for him._  
> - **cause chaos** — 혼란을 일으키다

## 4. China’s Tech Growth Creates Tension in Silicon Valley and Washington 

China’s fast **tech advances** are creating tension from Silicon Valley to the White House. Many American tech companies are worried about competition from China’s technology industry. This worry has led some companies to lobby the government to limit Chinese access to US technology. This situation may **cause chaos** between the two countries. Some experts think this could lead to a “mutual suspicion” where both sides distrust each other more. The rivalry is not just about business but also about national security. How the US handles this issue could **advance** new rules on technology sharing and trade.
