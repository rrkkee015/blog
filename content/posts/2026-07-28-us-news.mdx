## 1. Trump Administration Seeks Court Approval to Limit Mail-In Voting Before Midterms  


The Trump administration recently asked the Supreme Court to allow a new order that would limit mail-in voting before the upcoming midterm elections. This order targets certain rules about how mail-in ballots are handled. Supporters say it is to prevent fraud, but critics worry it could **lock out** many voters who prefer to vote by mail. Mail-in voting became more popular during the pandemic and is seen as a safe way to vote. The Supreme Court's decision could affect how easy or hard it is for people to vote remotely. This case is important because it could change election rules just weeks before voting begins. Many people are watching closely to see if the court will **pull back** on mail-in voting rights.

> - **lock out** — (어떤 권리나 접근을) 차단하다, 배제하다  
>   _Many voters felt **locked out** by the new voting rules._  
> - **pull back** — 철회하다, 후퇴하다  
>   _The government decided to **pull back** its plan after protests._  
> - **target** — 목표로 삼다, 겨냥하다  
>   _The policy **targets** mail-in ballots to prevent fraud._

## 2. New Federal Rule Sends More Asylum Cases to Immigration Courts  


The U.S. government has created a new rule that sends more asylum cases directly to immigration courts. This change means many migrants must go through a longer and harder legal process. Experts say this will likely **push migrants toward deportation** because immigration courts are often busy and slow. Asylum seekers who want to stay in the U.S. must prove they face danger in their home countries. The new rule makes this process more difficult and could lead to more people being sent back. Immigration advocates are worried this will affect vulnerable groups. It is a big change in how the government handles immigration cases.  

> ### 영어 표현 학습  
> - **push toward** — ~쪽으로 밀어내다, 유도하다  
>   _The new policy **pushes migrants toward** quicker deportation._  
> - **asylum seeker** — 망명 신청자  
>   _An **asylum seeker** asks for protection in another country._  
> - **legal process** — 법적 절차  
>   _The **legal process** can take many months or years._

## 3. Two Million Workers Locked Out of the Improving Job Market  


Even though the U.S. economy is adding jobs, about two million workers are still **locked out** of the job market. These workers face challenges like lack of experience, skills, or other barriers. The job market is improving, but not everyone benefits equally. Many industries want to hire, but some people find it hard to get a job. Experts say this gap could affect economic growth and increase inequality. The government and businesses may need to find ways to help these workers get back to work soon. This story shows that even a growing economy does not guarantee jobs for everyone.

> - **job market** — 취업 시장, 일자리 시장

> - **locked out** — 배제되다, 접근하지 못하다

> - **economic growth** — 경제 성장

## 4. Silicon Valley Debates Closing Borders to Chinese AI Technology  


Silicon Valley is divided over whether to **close the borders** to Chinese artificial intelligence (AI) technology and companies. Some leaders want to protect U.S. technology from foreign competition. Others worry this could slow innovation and harm business relationships. The debate comes as AI grows quickly and becomes very important worldwide. Closing borders means restricting who can share or access AI tools and information. This decision could affect the future of AI development and international cooperation. Many experts say finding the right balance between security and openness is very hard. The outcome will shape how the U.S. leads in AI technology.  

> ### 영어 표현 학습  
> - **close the borders** — 국경을 닫다, 출입을 제한하다  
>   _The government plans to **close the borders** to some foreign tech firms._  
> - **foreign competition** — 외국 경쟁  
>   _Local companies worry about **foreign competition** in the market._  
> - **innovation** — 혁신, 새로운 아이디어  
>   _Strong **innovation** drives economic success._
