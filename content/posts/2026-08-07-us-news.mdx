## 1. Trump’s New Plan to Limit Birthright Citizenship 

Former President Trump has tried again to change the rule about birthright citizenship in the US. Birthright citizenship means that any baby born in the US automatically becomes a citizen. Trump signed new executive orders to try to stop this rule. He says this will help control immigration and protect American jobs. However, many experts and politicians say this move may not be legal and could face court challenges. This issue is very important because it affects many families and the future of immigration policy in the US. We will have to wait and see if these orders will actually change the current law.

## 2. What Trump’s Public Charge Rule Means for Immigrants 

The US government has a new rule called the “public charge” rule. This rule makes it harder for some immigrants to get green cards if they use certain public benefits like Medicaid or food stamps. The government says this rule will encourage immigrants to be more financially independent. But many immigrant groups worry this will make life harder for poor families and could stop people from getting needed help. This rule changes how immigration officers decide who can stay in the US. It is important because it affects many people’s future and health in America.

## 3. US Economy Adds 79,000 Jobs in July 

The US economy added 79,000 new jobs in July, according to a recent report. This number is lower than some expected but shows that hiring is still happening. Some industries like technology and healthcare are growing, but others are slower. Experts say the job market is changing and may face challenges ahead. Teen hiring this summer was low, which may show that young workers are finding it harder to get jobs. Overall, the job market is still strong but could slow down if the economy faces more problems.

## 4. Silicon Valley Debates AI Rules and US-China Relations 

Silicon Valley is now in the middle of a big debate about how the US should control artificial intelligence (AI) technology. Some companies want fewer rules to help innovation, while others support stronger government controls to protect security. The situation is complicated by tensions between the US and China. Some worry that strict rules may cause **mutual suspicion** between the two countries and hurt business. The White House is trying to find the right balance but faces challenges from different groups. This debate will affect the future of AI and international technology relations.
