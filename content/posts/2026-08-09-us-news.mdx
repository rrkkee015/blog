## 1. Iran Says Strait of Hormuz Deal Is Close but Not Enough to Open Waterway  


Iran has announced that a deal about the Strait of Hormuz is almost ready. The Strait of Hormuz is a very important waterway for world oil shipping. Iran controls part of it. Recently, there have been tensions because Iran did not allow some ships to pass. The new deal aims to make it safer and open for ships again. However, Iran says the deal is **not enough** yet for them to fully open the waterway. This matters because many countries depend on oil passing through this strait. If the waterway stays closed or dangerous, it could cause problems for the global economy. People will watch closely to see if the deal improves soon.

## 2. ICE Plans to Equip All Field Officers with Body Cameras by August  


The U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agency said it will give body cameras to every field officer by the end of August. Body cameras are small devices officers wear to record their work. This move aims to increase **transparency** and trust between officers and the public. The cameras can show what happens during immigration enforcement actions. ICE says this will help improve officer behavior and protect civil rights. Some people hope this will reduce conflicts and complaints. This new policy is part of a larger effort to modernize immigration enforcement.

## 3. July Job Reports Show Mixed Signs in U.S. Labor Market  


The U.S. job market had mixed results in July. One report from ADP said private companies hired fewer workers than expected. Another report showed job losses and some negative revisions to previous months. This means the labor market might be getting weaker. However, a different report said the economy added 79,000 jobs in July, which is a positive sign. Experts say the job market is still changing and not very strong yet. Workers and employers are watching closely because jobs are important for the economy. The government will release more data soon to show how things are going.

## 4. Silicon Valley Divides Over Closing Borders to Chinese AI  


Silicon Valley experts are split about stopping Chinese AI technology from entering the U.S. Some believe closing borders to Chinese AI companies is necessary for national security. Others worry this will hurt innovation and cause **mutual suspicion** between the U.S. and China. AI is a fast-growing technology that both countries want to lead. The debate is about how to balance security and progress. Many companies in Silicon Valley work with AI from around the world. What happens next will affect global technology and business.

> ### 영어 표현 학습  
> - **divided over** — ~에 대해 의견이 나뉘다  
>   _People are **divided over** the new policy._  
> - **closing borders** — 국경을 닫다, 입국 금지하다  
>   _The country is **closing borders** to certain products._  
> - **mutual suspicion** — 상호 의심  
>   _There is **mutual suspicion** between the two groups._  
> - **balance security and progress** — 보안과 발전을 균형 있게 하다  
>   _The company tries to **balance security and progress**._  
> - **fast-growing technology** — 빠르게 성장하는 기술  
>   _AI is a **fast-growing technology** worldwide._
