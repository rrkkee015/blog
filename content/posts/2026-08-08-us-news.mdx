## 1. Court Blocks Trump’s Plan to Build White House Ballroom 

A U.S. appeals court has ruled that former President Trump cannot build a ballroom at the White House without Congress’s permission. Trump wanted to add this large space for events and meetings. However, the court said that building such a space needs **congressional approval** because it involves government property and funds. This decision is important because it limits the president’s power to change White House structures without the lawmakers’ agreement. Trump’s team may try to appeal this ruling or find other ways to pursue the construction. For now, building the ballroom is on hold until Congress agrees. This case shows how government branches check each other’s power.

## 2. ICE Threatens Sheriffs Over Inmate Access Rules 

The U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agency has warned sheriffs that they could face legal action if they do not give ICE access to certain inmates. ICE wants to check if inmates are undocumented immigrants who should be detained or deported. Some sheriffs have refused or limited access because of concerns about local rights and trust with communities. ICE says it will **prosecute** those who block access, which increases tension between local law enforcement and the federal agency. This issue is important because it affects how immigration laws are enforced and how local police cooperate with ICE. The conflict may lead to more legal battles or new rules.

## 3. U.S. Job Market Shows Signs of Weakening in July 

In July, the U.S. job market showed signs of weakness with unexpected job losses and negative revisions to previous reports. Employers cut about 23,000 jobs, which was surprising because many expected growth. This slowdown has made economists and investors worry about the economy’s strength. Some believe it could affect future decisions about interest rates and government policies. The job losses may also have political effects, as they come before important elections. Overall, the data suggests the labor market is not as strong as before, making the economic future uncertain.

## 4. Silicon Valley Divided Over Closing Borders to Chinese AI 

Silicon Valley is split about whether the U.S. should stop Chinese companies from developing or sharing artificial intelligence (AI) technology. Some people want to **close the borders** to Chinese AI to protect national security and keep American technology safe. Others worry this could cause more **mutual suspicion** and hurt business cooperation between the two countries. The debate is important because AI is a powerful technology shaping the future. How the U.S. handles this issue will affect global tech development and political relations. The discussion continues as both sides try to balance security with innovation.

> - **mutual suspicion** — 상호 의심  
>   _There is **mutual suspicion** between the two companies after the dispute._  
> - **split** — 의견이 갈리다

> - **balance** — 균형을 맞추다
