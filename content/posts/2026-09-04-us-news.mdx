## 1. Judge Rules Trump’s 75-Country Visa Ban Was Illegal 

A federal judge recently decided that the Trump administration’s ban on visas from 75 countries was **illegal**. The ban stopped many people from entering the US, citing security concerns. However, the judge said the government did not follow the correct process before making the ban. This ruling is important because it affects many immigrants and travelers who were blocked. It also shows how courts can **hold the government accountable** for its policies. The decision could lead to changes in how future visa rules are made. People affected by the ban may now have a chance to apply for visas again.

> - **illegal** — 불법의  
>   _The judge said the new rule was **illegal** because it broke the law._  
> - **hold the government accountable** — 정부에 책임을 묻다  
>   _Citizens want to **hold the government accountable** for its actions._  
> - **policy** — 정책  
>   _The new **policy** will affect many people._  
> - **affect** — 영향을 미치다  
>   _The visa ban will **affect** thousands of travelers._

## 2. US Job Growth Expected to Improve in August 

Experts say the US job market will **rebound** in August after some slow months. The unemployment rate is expected to stay steady at 4.1%. This means many people who want work will still have a good chance to find jobs. A **cool job market** means hiring is slower, but the forecast shows growth may return soon. Job growth is important because it affects the economy and people's daily lives. If more people work, they can spend money and help businesses grow. The government and companies will watch these numbers closely.

> ### 영어 표현 학습
> - **rebound** — 다시 회복하다  
>   _The economy will **rebound** after the slow period._  
> - **steady** — 안정된, 변함없는  
>   _The unemployment rate stayed **steady** this month._  
> - **job market** — 취업 시장  
>   _The **job market** is competitive for new graduates._  
> - **forecast** — 예측  
>   _Experts made a positive **forecast** for job growth._

## 3. States Push Back Against Tech Lobby on AI Rules 

Several US states are creating their own rules for artificial intelligence (AI), even though big tech companies want to set the standards themselves. The tech lobby wants fewer rules to keep innovation fast. But states say the **landscape has changed dramatically** with AI’s risks and benefits. They want to protect people from problems like privacy loss and unfair decisions made by AI. This fight shows how powerful AI is becoming and how governments are trying to **keep up** with technology. The result could lead to different AI laws in different states, which might be confusing for companies.

> ### 영어 표현 학습
> - **landscape has changed dramatically** — 상황이 크게 변했다  
>   _Since AI grew, the **landscape has changed dramatically**._  
> - **tech lobby** — 기술 로비 (기술 산업 단체)  
>   _The **tech lobby** tries to influence government decisions._  
> - **keep up** — (변화에) 뒤처지지 않다, 따라가다  
>   _Governments must **keep up** with fast technology changes._  
> - **innovation** — 혁신  
>   _New **innovation** can improve many industries._

## 4. Trump Administration Seeks Supreme Court Approval for Mail-In Ballot Rules 

The Trump administration asked the Supreme Court to allow stricter rules on mail-in ballots. Mail-in ballots let people vote without going to a polling place. The administration wants to limit who can use mail-in voting, saying it will prevent fraud. But opponents say these rules make it harder for people to vote, especially during the pandemic. This case is important because it affects how easy or hard it is to vote in US elections. The Supreme Court’s decision will shape future voting rights and election rules.

> ### 영어 표현 학습
> - **mail-in ballot** — 우편 투표  
>   _Many people use **mail-in ballots** to vote safely._  
> - **limit** — 제한하다  
>   _The government wants to **limit** mail-in voting._  
> - **prevent fraud** — 사기를 막다  
>   _Rules are made to **prevent fraud** in elections._  
> - **opponent** — 반대자  
>   _**Opponents** say the new law is unfair._
